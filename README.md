# Sales Performance Analysis using SQL & R

End-to-end analysis of transactional retail sales data: **SQL** for extraction, cleaning and KPI
engineering; **R** for exploratory analysis, statistical testing, forecasting and
profit-improvement scenarios.

> **Data note.** `data/sales_raw.csv` is a *synthetic*, deliberately messy Superstore-style dataset
> (12,504 order lines, 2021-2024, 700 customers) generated by `data/generate_data.py`. The findings
> below describe this dataset only. To analyse real data, replace `sales_raw.csv` with your extract
> (same 16 columns) and re-run `Rscript R/run_all.R`. Nothing else changes.

## Pipeline

```
data/sales_raw.csv ──► R/01_build_db.R ──► SQLite (data/sales.sqlite)
                         │ sql/01_clean.sql   de-dupe, standardise, parse dates, validate, audit trail
                         │ sql/02_kpis.sql    KPI views using CTEs + window functions (LAG, RANK, NTILE)
                         ▼
        R/02_eda_kpis.R   KPI tables + 8 charts (trend, growth, category, region, discount, seasonality, Pareto)
        R/03_stats.R      Spearman, Welch t-test, Kruskal-Wallis, OLS driver regression
        R/04_forecast.R   Hold-out backtest (SNaive / ETS / ARIMA / STL+ETS) -> 2025 forecast with intervals
        R/05_recommendations.R  Discount-cap scenarios, loss-making pockets
                         ▼
                  output/figures (12 PNGs)   output/tables (CSV)
```

## Run it

```bash
python3 data/generate_data.py          # optional: regenerate sample data
Rscript R/run_all.R                    # run everything from the project root
```
R packages: DBI, RSQLite, dplyr, tidyr, ggplot2, scales, lubridate, readr, forecast, zoo, ggrepel, broom, purrr.

Also fixed: mixed `dd/mm/yyyy` vs ISO dates, inconsistent region/category casing and whitespace.

## KPIs evaluated

Sales growth (YoY), profit margin, category / sub-category performance, regional performance,
discount impact, average order value, customer value (RFM-style quintiles), segment performance,
seasonality, customer concentration.

## Key findings (on the sample data)

| Area | Finding |
|---|---|
| Growth | Sales grew from $1.79M (2021) to $2.70M (2024), about 14.7% CAGR. Margin improved from 9.5% to 10.5%. |
| Discounts | Lines discounted above 20% have a negative combined margin (-4.8% at 21-30%, -14.4% at 31-50%, -26.5% above 50%). Margin on undiscounted lines is 14.8%. Spearman correlation of discount vs margin is -0.63. |
| Categories | Technology/Copiers earns 24.9% margin and about 96% of total profit. Tables (-8.1%), Bookcases (-3.8%) and Machines (-1.0%) lose money. |
| Regions | Central has the lowest margin (7.9%) and the deepest average discount (19.3%). Furniture is loss-making in every region. |
| Seasonality | November + December account for about 25% of annual sales. |
| Customers | The top 20% of customers generate 37% of sales (moderate concentration). |
| Drivers | OLS on margin (R² = 0.77): discount has a strong non-linear negative effect; category dominates regional effects; segment is not significant. |
| Forecast | Best backtest model on 2024 hold-out: STL + ETS (MAPE 9.2% vs 16.2% for seasonal naive). 2025 forecast: **$2.93M (+8.6%)**, 95% interval $2.32M-$3.55M. |

## Recommendations

1. **Introduce a discount governance rule.** Cap discounts at 30%, with approval above that. In the
   FY2024 scenario analysis this adds about **$124k profit (+45%)** if 80% of volume on re-priced
   lines is retained, and about $105k even at 60% retention.
2. **Fix or re-price the Furniture line** (Tables, Bookcases), where margin is negative in every
   region, and review Machines pricing and cost-to-serve.
3. **Target Central region discounting first.** It combines the heaviest discounts with the weakest margin.
4. **Plan inventory and staffing for Q4** and use the forecast intervals for revenue planning.
5. **Protect the top customer quintile** with account management, and test retention offers that do not rely on deep discounts.

Scenario assumptions (re-pricing lines at the cap, cost unchanged, a stated retention rate) are explicit
in `R/05_recommendations.R`. They are directional estimates, not guarantees.

## Limitations

- Sample data is synthetic; real data will have different relationships.
- Forecast uses only 48 months of history and no external drivers (promotions, macro, pricing).
- Discount scenarios assume costs are fixed and ignore price elasticity beyond the retention parameter.
- Statistical tests are on transaction lines, which are not fully independent (repeat customers and orders).
